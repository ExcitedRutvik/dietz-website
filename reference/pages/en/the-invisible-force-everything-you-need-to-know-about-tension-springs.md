# The invisible force: everything you need to know about tension springs

## What are tension springs?

Tension springs are resilient components that are used to maintain a controlled tension or preload and hold components together. They are made of wire that is wound in a spiral or wave-like configuration. This configuration enables the spring to store and release energy when a force is applied to it.

## How do tension springs work?

Tension springs work according to the principle of elastic deformation. When a tension spring is stretched or compressed, potential energy is stored in the spring. This energy is released when the force exerted on the spring decreases or is removed. This mechanism allows the spring to be used as a tensioning element to hold components firmly in position.

## Types and applications of tension springs

There are different types of tension springs, depending on requirements and applications:

### Compression springs

Compression springs are used to maintain an axial compression force. They are often used in applications such as valves, punching machines and tools to hold parts in place or ensure damping.

### Extension springs

Extension springs are known for their function as return springs. They are used in applications such as doors, gates and mechanical devices to generate a restoring force and return components to their original position.

### Torsion springs

Torsion springs are used to generate torque. They are used in devices such as clocks, door locks and flap mechanisms to enable controlled rotation or turning movement.

## Conclusion

Tension springs are an invisible force in industry and play a crucial role in securing and moving components. Whether it’s compression, tension or torsion, tension springs provide the necessary tension to ensure reliable performance.
